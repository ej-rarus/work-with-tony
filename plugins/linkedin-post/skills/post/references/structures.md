# Post Structures

Six skeletons that decide the shape of a post independently of its type. Pick one per post and record its id in the published file's frontmatter as `structure:`.

Rotation rule: read the `structure:` value of the 3 most recent published posts and pick one that is not among them. Never use the same structure twice in a row. A topic that strongly demands a specific structure is the only exception, and say so next to the draft.

If `$LINKEDIN_POST_HOME/structures.md` exists it replaces this file.

## scene
A concrete moment from the day opens the post. Then observation, then judgment, then one closing line of reflection.
- Open with a line of dialogue or a specific situation.
- Close with a feeling, not a lesson. No "the takeaway is".
- This is the default shape and the easiest to overuse. Ration it.

## rules
The decisions or principles come first as a numbered list. The prose after the list explains only the one contested item.
- 3 to 5 items, one sentence each.
- Explain why, not what. The list already said what.
- Readers save this shape. Make the list stand on its own.

## questions
No conclusion. The real questions the author hit, listed raw, one per line. The last question seeds the next post.
- 6 to 10 questions, no answers.
- One closing line only, such as "I stopped at nine."
- Use when the topic is unresolved. Do not fake resolution.

## contrarian
Quote the sentence everyone treats as settled in the first line, then defend the opposite.
- Open with "요즘은 ~가 정답처럼 들리지만" or an equivalent.
- Always limit the claim: in agencies, in client documents, for teams of this size. An unlimited contrarian claim is a rant.
- Check that the "settled" sentence is actually current. Arguing against a position nobody holds anymore is the failure mode.

## compare
A good example and a bad example, described on the same axes in the same order. The judgment appears only in the last paragraph.
- Same items for both sides: length, count of tables, number of decisions, and so on.
- Numbers make this shape. Get real ones.
- Never name the author of the bad example.

## short
300 to 500 characters. Two or three paragraphs. One observation.
- Show, do not explain.
- Two hashtags.
- Use after three long posts in a row, or when the idea is one sentence long.

## Choosing
- Unresolved topic → questions
- Decisions already made → rules
- The point is a disagreement with common advice → contrarian
- Two concrete artifacts on hand → compare
- Three posts over 1,000 characters just went out → short
- Otherwise → scene, but only if the last two were not scene
